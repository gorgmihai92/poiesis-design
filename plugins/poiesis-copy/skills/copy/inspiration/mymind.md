# mymind · https://mymind.com

Read 2026-10-17 (home page and manifesto). A private app for saving and finding notes, links and images.

## What it does well
- **Define the product by what the reader stops doing.** The headline pairs what you keep doing with what you no
  longer have to do, so the benefit is a relief rather than a feature. (Practice for: landing page.)
- **Belief before product.** The manifesto names a problem the reader already feels, then the product's answer,
  and only then the product; the product arrives as a welcome, not a pitch. (Practice for: landing page, about.)
- **Promises as refusals.** A short list of what it will never do (ads, social features, metrics), each a
  fragment of a few words: concrete, checkable, and it answers privacy doubts at once. (Practice for: landing page.)
- **Two speeds.** Promises and headings in short parallel fragments; explanations in one or two full sentences.
  The rhythm makes the page calm to read. (Practice for: voice.)
- **"You" for the product, "we" for the promises.** The reader owns the space; the company speaks only where it
  commits to something. (Practice for: voice.)
- **Features named for the experience** ("Serendipity", "Top of Mind"), each explained by what it does for you.
  (Practice for: product copy.)

## Cautions
- "Magically organized" names a benefit without saying how it works; give enough mechanism to be believed (Q6).
- A branded feature name alone is the team's word, not the reader's; pair it with a plain one (L1).
- "The first and only" needs proof or goes (Q1).
