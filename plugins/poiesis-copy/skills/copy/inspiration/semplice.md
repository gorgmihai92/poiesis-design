# Semplice · https://www.semplice.com

Read 2026-10-17 (home page). A portfolio builder for designers, on WordPress.

## What it does well
- **Peer to peer.** It speaks to designers in their own vocabulary (grid, breakpoints, variable type) and doesn't
  explain what they know; the reader feels addressed as a colleague. (Practice for: voice, expert audiences.)
- **One idea carried through the page.** A single word from the hero comes back in later headings, so the page
  reads as one argument. (Practice for: landing page.)
- **People before features.** Who already uses it, by name and with their work, comes before the feature list;
  the reader's peers make the case. (Practice for: landing page.)
- **Kicker, heading, one paragraph.** Each section has a small label, a short declarative heading and a few lines
  of support; easy to scan, nothing to wade through. (Practice for: landing page.)
- **Old way, new way.** A feature is introduced by what it used to take ("you used to need developers"), so the
  benefit is felt, not stated. (Practice for: product copy, landing page.)
- **Price after the case.** Editions come late, once the reader wants it. (Practice for: landing page.)

## Cautions
- Generic "Learn more" links on features; name the destination (B2).
- "The only building experience made for designers" is a superlative to prove or drop (Q1).
- Insider vocabulary only works for an insider audience; for anyone else it is jargon (L1).
