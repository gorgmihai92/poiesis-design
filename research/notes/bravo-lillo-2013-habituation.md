# Your attention please: designing security-decision UIs to make genuine risks harder to ignore (Bravo-Lillo, Cranor, Downs, Komanduri, Reeder, Schechter & Sleeper, 2013)

- **Source:** Proceedings of SOUPS 2013 · https://doi.org/10.1145/2501604.2501610 (pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** local only (author copy)
- **Licence:** © authors / ACM: local only.
- **Evidence:** empirical · three between-subjects experiments (p. 1).

## What it says
- People quickly become habituated to ignore dialogs whose earlier instances held no reason for concern (p. 11).
- Dialogs that briefly hold back the risky action and point to the information that matters reduced uninformed
  decisions, at the cost of delay (p. 11).

## What it means for the plugin
- Confirmation dialogs lose their effect when they appear for routine actions. Prefer undo for routine actions;
  keep confirmation for rare, irreversible ones, and make it name the specific thing at stake.
