# Choice overload: a conceptual review and meta-analysis (Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman, 2015)

- **Source:** Journal of Consumer Psychology 25(2): 333–358 · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002
- **File:** none (abstract and summaries only)
- **Licence:** © Elsevier: closed.
- **Evidence:** meta-analysis, 99 observations from 53 studies (7,202 participants) · **abstract only**.

## What it says (from the abstract and summaries)
- More options hurt choice only under conditions: difficult decisions, complex option sets, uncertain preferences,
  a wish to minimize effort. An earlier meta-analysis (Scheibehenne et al. 2010) found a near-zero average effect.

## What it means for the plugin
- "Fewer options" is not a universal rule; reduce or structure options when the decision is hard or the person
  does not know what they want (good defaults, grouping, comparison).
