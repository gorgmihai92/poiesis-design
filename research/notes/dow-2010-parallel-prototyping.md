## What it says
- Participants each designed a web banner advertisement for a student design magazine. Both groups made five
  prototypes and a final version, with the same amount of critique in the same time. The parallel group made three
  prototypes before any critique, then two more; the serial group received critique after each one (p. 3).
- Who took part: 33 people, mean age 22, three quarters of them students, 14 with some experience of ad or graphic
  design, none a professional designer (p. 3).
- The final ads ran for 15 days. Parallel ads drew 445.0 clicks per million impressions and serial ads 397.9
  (p. 5). Raters gave parallel ads 24.4 of 50 and serial ads 21.7 (p. 6).
- Each person's prototypes were rated for how similar they were, from 1 to 7: 3.25 in the serial group and 2.78 in
  the parallel group, so parallel prototypes differed more from each other (p. 6).
- How different a person's prototypes were did not predict how well their final ad did (p. 6).
- Belief in one's own ability to do the task rose by 2.5 points in the parallel group and 0.4 in the serial group
  (p. 7). Eight of 17 serial participants called the critique negative; none of the 16 parallel participants did
  (p. 8).
- Participants with design experience did better than novices and made prototypes that were more alike (p. 7).
- The authors' reading: seeing critique on several ideas at once invites comparison, and critique after each idea
  invites refining it (p. 7–8). They ask for studies of other tasks, longer periods and teams (p. 9).

## What it means for the plugin
- `explore` makes its variants before any of them is judged, and they are judged together. That is the condition
  the study tested: the timing of critique, with the number of prototypes held the same.
- What the study does not show: that this holds for interfaces, for experienced designers working over weeks, or
  for a model generating the variants. It was one task, one session and people who were mostly students.
- More difference between variants is not shown to give a better result (p. 6). The skill asks for different
  variants so the space is seen, and claims no more.
