# A comparison of static, adaptive, and adaptable menus (Findlater & McGrenere, 2004)

- **Source:** Proceedings of CHI 2004, pp. 89–96 · https://doi.org/10.1145/985692.985704 (pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** local only (author copy)
- **Licence:** © ACM: local only.
- **Evidence:** empirical · controlled lab study, 27 participants (p. 1).

## What it says
- Static menus were significantly faster than menus that rearranged themselves by use; menus the person
  customized were as fast as static ones (when not used first) and preferred by most (55%) (p. 1, 7–8). The static
  menu was an optimal split menu built from known frequencies; 30% preferred the adaptive one (p. 7).

## What it means for the plugin
- Do not reorder existing items on their own; put usage-based suggestions in a separate, labelled area and let
  people pin or reorder (see also Gajos et al. 2008).
