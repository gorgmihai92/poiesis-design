- **Read:** part of the text: p. 1, 4–5 and 8–9 of 10 (abstract, introduction, the section on early designs, "What

## What it says
- Usability evaluation is valuable in many situations and harmful when done by rule instead of by thought. In early
  design it can mute ideas that do not fit current interface norms (p. 1).
- An early design is a sketch: it shows the essence of an idea and is rough elsewhere. A working sketch is easily
  taken for a prototype, which stands for a finished product (p. 4).
- Testing a sketch as if it were a prototype finds many problems and can end the idea, most easily when a new
  design is set against a conservative one (p. 5).
- The usual methods look for problems, which are easier to measure than benefits, so they tilt early decisions
  toward what is already familiar (p. 5).
- Evaluation of usability belongs after several ideas have been generated, critiqued and considered (p. 5).
- For early work the authors point to the design critique of studio disciplines: the designer says why the design
  is as it is, and others say what they like and dislike, question its assumptions and suggest changes (p. 8). At
  the least an author should say why things were done, what else was considered, what was learned, which problems
  are expected, how it sits with prior work and what comes next (p. 8).
- Dropping usability testing altogether would be as wrong as applying it everywhere (p. 9).

## What it means for the plugin
- In `explore` nothing is audited or tested against rules; the `ui-auditor` and `ux-tester` agents do not run on
  sketches. A sketch is judged by a written critique that answers the questions on p. 8, and the pattern card keeps
  the answers.
- This is an argument by experienced practitioners, not a measured effect. It does not show that a critique
  chooses better than a test would.
- The essay's own limit applies here too: once a pattern is adopted for a product, the usual checks are the right
  tool again.
