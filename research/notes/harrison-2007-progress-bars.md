# Rethinking the progress bar (Harrison, Amento, Kuznetsov & Bell, 2007)

- **Source:** Proceedings of UIST 2007, pp. 115–118 · https://doi.org/10.1145/1294211.1294231 (pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** local only (author copy)
- **Licence:** © ACM: local only.
- **Evidence:** empirical · 22 participants, paired comparisons of nine progress behaviours, all lasting 5.5 s (p. 2–4).

## What it says
- How a bar moves changes how long the wait feels (p. 1, 4). People strongly dislike pauses, especially towards
  the end; slow or variable stages are tolerated best at the start (p. 4).
- Bars that progress steadily and finish briskly were preferred (p. 3–4). All bars lasted 5.5 s; longer waits not
  tested (p. 4).

## What it means for the plugin
- Run the slow or uncertain stages first; avoid stalls near the end. The authors also suggest playing down early
  progress and speeding up the end (p. 4); the plugin does not adopt that: progress reflects real work.
