# When and why defaults influence decisions: a meta-analysis of default effects (Jachimowicz, Duncan, Weber & Johnson, 2019)

- **Source:** Behavioural Public Policy 3(2): 159–186 · https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2018.43
- **File:** none (abstract only)
- **Licence:** © Cambridge University Press: closed.
- **Evidence:** meta-analysis, 58 studies, about 73,700 participants · **abstract only**.

## What it says (from the abstract)
- People are more likely to choose a pre-selected option; the overall effect is substantial (d = 0.68) but varies:
  most studies positive, some null, two negative. Larger in consumer settings; stronger when the default is read
  as a recommendation or as the status quo.

## What it means for the plugin
- A default is a decision the app makes for most people: it must serve them.
