# Dark patterns at scale: findings from a crawl of 11K shopping websites (Mathur, Acar, Friedman, Lucherini, Mayer, Chetty & Narayanan, 2019)

- **Source:** Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction 3 (CSCW), Article 81 · https://doi.org/10.1145/3359183 (pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** local only (arXiv copy)
- **Licence:** arXiv non-exclusive licence: local only.
- **Evidence:** empirical · about 53K product pages from about 11K shopping sites (p. 1).

## What it says
- Dark patterns: design choices that benefit the service by coercing, steering or deceiving users into decisions
  they would not make if fully informed (p. 2). Found on about 11% of the sites; 183 used deceptive messages (p. 27).
- Types (Table 1, p. 12): sneak into basket, hidden costs, hidden subscription, countdown timers, limited-time
  messages, confirmshaming, visual interference, trick questions, pressured selling, activity messages, unclear
  testimonials, low-stock and high-demand messages, hard to cancel, forced enrollment.

## What it means for the plugin
- A list of behaviours an app must not have, usable as a checklist by the tester.
