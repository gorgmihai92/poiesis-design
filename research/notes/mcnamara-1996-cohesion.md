# Are Good Texts Always Better? (McNamara, Kintsch, Songer & Kintsch, 1996)

- **Source:** Cognition and Instruction 14(1): 1–43 · https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1
- **File:** none
- **Licence:** closed; not stored.
- **Evidence:** empirical · small experiments with school students · **known from search summaries; paper not read**.

## What it says
- Readers with little background knowledge understood more from cohesive texts (explicit links between ideas);
  readers who knew more sometimes learned more from less cohesive ones (the reverse cohesion effect).

## What it means for the plugin
- "Short" must not strip the joints between ideas (L4). Shortness is a should, not a must.
