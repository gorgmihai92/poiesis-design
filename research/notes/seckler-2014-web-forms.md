# Designing usable web forms: empirical evaluation of web form improvement guidelines (Seckler, Heinz, Bargas-Avila, Opwis & Tuch, 2014)

- **Source:** Proceedings of CHI 2014, pp. 1275–1284 · https://doi.org/10.1145/2556288.2557265 (pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** local only (author copy)
- **Licence:** © ACM: local only.
- **Evidence:** empirical · controlled eye-tracking experiment, N = 65, real company forms original vs improved.

## What it says
- Applying 20 form guidelines together gave faster completion, fewer submission attempts, fewer eye movements and
  higher satisfaction (p. 1). The effect is of the whole set; single guidelines were not tested alone (p. 1, 9).
- Highest-rated guidelines by 14 experts (Table 2, p. 3): never clear completed fields (5.00); order options in an
  intuitive sequence; provide a confirmation page; clear error-message text; show all error messages after sending
  the form; no reset buttons; state a required format in advance; disable submit once clicked; separate required
  from optional fields; do not ask for unnecessary input; accept answers in familiar formats.

## What it means for the plugin
- A form pattern with evidence as a set: keep input, format in advance, errors after submit next to the field,
  no reset, submit disabled after the click, only necessary fields, confirmation of what was sent.
